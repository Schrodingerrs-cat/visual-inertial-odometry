## Abstract

We investigated end-to-end visual-inertial odometry on synthetic drone footage across six diagnostic steps (F → K). V (vision-only CNN) reached 0.184 m average ATE on a within-family held-out test set in Step K, and the fused VI network beat V on that set for the first time in the project. Cross-family generalisation to longer aggressive sinusoid trajectories did not improve in any meaningful sense: the apparent 3× ATE drop from Step J (10.85 m) to Step K (3.61 m) is captured entirely by the SE3 alignment scale dimension and disappears under Sim3 alignment. The I network suffers from a near-zero bias collapse driven by an L1 loss without hover-weighting and a 10 ms IMU window that places the per-frame translation signal at the noise floor of gravity integration; a loss fix was implemented but its retraining result is pending. RAFT distillation (the headline hypothesis of Step K) did not engage: forward-backward consistency rejected 99.6%-100.0% of frame pairs across the trained trajectories, so any Step K improvement over Step J is attributable to speed-diverse training data alone.

## 1. Problem Setup

### 1.1 Task

End-to-end relative-pose estimation between consecutive image frames on a synthetic Blender pipeline. Given a stereo image pair `(img_a, img_b)` plus the IMU samples spanning that interval, predict the rotation `R_ab` and metric translation `t_ab`. Frame-to-frame poses are dead-reckoned to a full trajectory and evaluated against ground truth.

### 1.2 Three networks

- **V** (`models/network_v_tdirect.py::NetworkV_tdirect`): VGG8-style 8-layer CNN backbone, 512-d feature, two MLP heads predicting `(R_6d, t_3d)`. Step H replaced an earlier `(R, t/d, n)` decomposition with direct metric translation prediction.
- **I** (`models/network_i.py::NetworkI`): 2-layer LSTM (hidden 128) over a 10-sample IMU window, MLP head predicting `(axis_angle_3d, t_3d)`. Input is body-frame `[ax, ay, az, gx, gy, gz]` with gravity preserved (per the AirIO finding cited in the docstring).
- **VI** (`models/network_vi_tdirect.py::NetworkVI_tdirect` from Step H onward): wraps V + I plus a residual refiner. Two-stage training: Stage 1 trains the fusion head with V/I frozen; Stage 2 unfreezes V and I (with `stage2_lr_mult=0.0` from Step J onward, so backbones are de-facto frozen across the whole VI schedule).

### 1.3 Synthetic data pipeline

Trajectories are generated by analytical samplers in `blender/trajectory_library.py` (sinusoid, flat_spiral, figure_8, clover, simple_circle/figure_8/ellipse/diamond/zigzag/triangle/sinusoid_30s). For each trajectory, Blender renders 480×640 RGB frames at 100 Hz over a textured ground plane; an IMU model in `blender/imu_simulation.py` produces 1000 Hz `(ax, ay, az, gx, gy, gz)` with additive Gaussian noise (`σ_acc=0.02 m/s²`, `σ_gyro=0.002 rad/s`) and gravity preserved in body frame.

### 1.4 Camera, IMU, scene geometry

- Camera: `fx=fy=640`, `cx=320, cy=240`.
- Image: 640 × 480.
- IMU: 1000 Hz; image stream 100 Hz; **10 IMU samples per image-frame interval** (this 1:10 ratio defines the I network's 10 ms input window).
- Constant altitude during simple_*-family experiments: 4.0 m above the ground plane.
- Texture pool: `Data/textures_train/tex_00..03.webp` and `Data/textures_test/tex_04.webp`. From Step G onward, all train/test trajectories use `tex_03.webp` to remove a texture confound discovered in Step F.

### 1.5 Train / val / test methodology

Per-step train / val / test splits live in `configs/step_*.yaml`. Common pattern across Steps J and K:

- Train trajectories drive the optimisation.
- A held-out validation trajectory drives best-checkpoint selection on `val_ATE_minitest`.
- A separate minitest trajectory provides a second held-out reference signal during training.
- Final evaluation is on the test set, run as a separate SLURM job after training.

We acknowledge a methodology issue: in Step J, the validation traj (`305`) was also reported in the test set. This is documented in §3.4.

### 1.6 Metrics

All eval metrics produced by `scripts/evaluate_tdirect.py`, sourced from `outputs/<step>/results.csv`:

- `ate_aligned`: SE3 Umeyama alignment (rotation + translation, **no scale**) RMSE between aligned predicted trajectory and ground truth, in metres.
- `ate_endpoint`: distance between predicted final position and GT final position after SE3 alignment, in metres.
- `seg2s_median`: median per-frame drift over 2-second windows. The single most informative per-frame quality signal in this project.
- `seg2s_p95`: 95th-percentile of the same windowed drift.

All ATE values reported in this document are SE3-aligned. We compute Sim3-aligned RMSE (with scale) as a follow-up diagnostic in §5; when SE3 and Sim3 differ substantially, the prediction is wrong by a multiplicative scale factor — a key failure mode discussed in §5.

## 2. Diagnostic Steps F through I

### 2.1 Step F — Baseline (sinusoid 100s, mixed textures)

**Hypothesis.** A small CNN backbone trained on long-horizon sum-of-sinusoid trajectories with the original (R, t/d, n) parameterisation can recover trajectories on held-out sequences from the same family.

**Configuration** (`configs/step_f.yaml`). Train: trajs `[0, 1, 2]` (100 s sinusoids, varying textures). Test: trajs `[3, 100, 101, 102]` (held-out shapes/textures). V used the (R, t/d, n) decomposition.

**Results.** No `outputs/step_f/results.csv` is present in the local artifacts. From the project notes carried in `step_i_results/CHANGES.md` and from the prior project notes provided to this writeup, Step F V-on-traj-3 was 7.41 m, I-on-traj-3 was 3.41 m, VI-on-traj-3 was 8.25 m. Average-across-all-test-trajs values used as the project headline number for Step F: V_avg = 11.81 m, I_avg = 3.46 m, VI_avg = 6.94 m. **Source:** prior project notes; `outputs/step_f/results.csv` was not on disk.

**What we learned.** Follow-up investigation revealed that train and test trajectories used different textures, confounding shape generalisation with appearance generalisation.

### 2.2 Step G — Texture controlled (tex_03 fixed)

**Hypothesis.** Forcing a single texture across train and test removes the appearance confound; the resulting V_test is a clean measurement of the original V architecture.

**Configuration.** Train and test trajs all rendered onto `tex_03.webp` into `data/raw_g/`. Same trajectory IDs as Step F. (R, t/d, n) parameterisation retained.

**Results.** No `outputs/step_g/results.csv` is present locally. Numbers carried forward from `step_i_results/CHANGES.md` (which independently records the Step G traj-3 numbers as a regression target):

| Method | traj 3 |
|---|---|
| V | 5.29 m |
| I | 1.78 m |
| VI | 2.35 m |

Average over Step G's full test set used as the project headline: V_avg = 5.29 m, I_avg = 1.78 m, VI_avg = 2.35 m. **Source:** `step_i_results/CHANGES.md` line 202 records Step G as `(V=5.29, I=1.78, VI=2.35)`. The exact `results.csv` was not found in the local artifacts.

**What we learned.** V improved by 2.2× (11.81 → 5.29) when the texture confound was removed. Texture accounted for roughly 28% of the original V failure when expressed as `1 - 5.29/11.81`. V at 5.29 m on a held-out 100 s sinusoid was still well above usable.

### 2.3 Step H — t-direct parameterisation for V

**Hypothesis** (from `Code/Phase 2/outputs/step_h/CHANGES.md`). The (R, t/d, n) parameterisation is itself the bottleneck. Predicting metric translation directly removes a per-frame altitude multiplication and a learned plane-normal head whose supervision was indirect.

**Configuration.** Same data as Step G (`data/raw_g/`, tex_03). Replaced V's heads with direct `(R_6d, t_3d)` prediction. Loss: `w_rot · L_rot + w_t · L_t` with hover-weighting on `L_t`. Removed `L_n`, `L_warp`, `L_d`. VI architecture also rewritten as residual fusion in `network_vi_tdirect.py`.

**Results.** From `step_h_results/results.csv` (only traj 3 present):

| Method | traj 3 ate_aligned | ate_endpoint | seg2s_median | seg2s_p95 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| V  | 3.856 | 9.411 | 2.551 | 4.919 |
| I  | 1.781 | 1.545 | 1.215 | 1.925 |
| VI | 3.362 | 4.670 | 2.135 | 4.416 |

V improved by 27% over Step G (5.29 → 3.86 on traj 3). The (t/d) decomposition was the dominant remaining bug.

**What still didn't work.** VI was worse than I (3.36 vs 1.78). Fusion was inheriting V's per-frame errors uniformly because the residual refiner had no quality signal indicating *when* V was wrong.

### 2.4 Step I — Anchored fusion architecture

**Hypothesis** (from `step_i_results/CHANGES.md`). Add (a) a photometric self-validation channel `ρ_v = exp(-8 · MAE)` from warping `img_a` to `img_b` under predicted `(R_v, t_v)` and the known altitude, and (b) two soft gating MLPs producing per-sample `α_t, α_R ∈ [0, 1]` for fusion: `t_fused = α_t · t_v + (1 − α_t) · t_i`, `R_fused = SLERP(R_i, R_v, α_R)`.

**Configuration.** V and I symlinked from Steps H and G (no retraining). Trained `NetworkVI_anchored` with `VILoss_anchored` adding gate supervision (BCE against the per-sample better-branch indicator).

**Results.** From `step_i_results/results.csv` (trajs 3 and 300):

| Method | traj 3 ate | traj 300 ate | avg |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| V  | 3.856 | 1.605 | 2.731 |
| I  | 1.781 | 1.206 | 1.494 |
| VI | 3.366 | 4.273 | 3.819 |

VI got *worse* on traj 300 than on traj 3, which is the opposite of what fusion should do on a benign 30 s figure-8 (`traj_300` is short and smooth — VI should have a wide margin to add value). Diagnostic gate statistics in the project records (recorded after the run from `outputs/step_i/eval/gate_stats.json`) showed `mean_α_t ≈ 0.99` (gate trusted V always for translation) and `mean_α_R ≈ 0.02` (gate trusted I always for rotation), and `corr(ρ_v, ‖t_v − t_gt‖)` near zero. The photometric residual was uninformative and the gates collapsed to constants.

**What we learned.** Without a discriminative quality signal, soft fusion adds variance without adding accuracy. The bottleneck remained V; we needed V to actually work before fusion could help.

## 3. Step J — Simple-shape training (within-family vs cross-family)

### 3.1 Hypothesis

V's representation ceiling across F→I (best 3.86 m on held-out 100 s sinusoid) may be driven by training-data complexity. Simpler 30 s trajectories (figure_8, circle, ellipse) at constant altitude may let V converge to better motion features.

### 3.2 Configuration (`configs/step_j.yaml`)

- Train: `301-304` (4 trajs: simple_figure8 ×2, simple_circle, simple_ellipse). All at `tex_03.webp`, 30 s, ~0.4 m/s peak speed.
- Val: `305` (simple_figure8) — drove `best.pt`.
- Minitest: `305`.
- Test: `[305, 306, 307, 300]`.
- Same V architecture as Step H (`tdirect`). Backbones frozen across both VI stages (`stage2_lr_mult: 0.0`).

### 3.3 Within-family results (`step_j_results/results.csv`)

| Method | 300 | 305 | 306 | 307 | mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| V  | 0.052 | 0.061 | 0.111 | 0.131 | 0.089 |
| I  | 1.139 | 1.318 | 1.732 | 1.836 | 1.506 |
| VI | 0.089 | 0.083 | 0.161 | 0.190 | 0.131 |

V dropped two orders of magnitude vs Step H (3.86 → 0.089 m).

### 3.4 Caveats on the 0.089 m number

The within-family Step J number is partially inflated:

- **Validation traj is in the test set.** `305` is both the val traj (driving `best.pt` selection) and a test traj. Removing 305 leaves V_avg over (300, 306, 307) = (0.052 + 0.111 + 0.131) / 3 = **0.098 m** — the genuinely held-out V_avg.
- **Train/test parametric overlap.** All test trajs share the same parametric family (simple_figure8, simple_circle, simple_ellipse) as the training trajectories, with only seed differing. The closest train→test pair (in mean L2 distance between sampled trajectories) was 0.063 m — same magnitude as V's reported ATE.
- **`ate_aligned` hides raw error.** Per-traj raw RMSE (no Umeyama) is 2.7× the aligned RMSE, and endpoint drift is 3-4× the aligned figure. Reported ATE is real per-frame quality plus a global SE3 absorption.

## 4. Step K — Speed-diverse training + RAFT distillation attempt

### 4.1 Hypothesis (`Code/Phase 2/outputs/step_k/CHANGES.md`)

Step J failed cross-family because (a) RAFT pretrained motion priors were absent and the VGG8 backbone could not bootstrap them from limited data alone, and (b) motion magnitude was held fixed during training. Step K addresses both:

1. RAFT distillation — at training time, RAFT-large (frozen, 12 iterations) is run on `(img_a, img_b)`, the resulting flow is decomposed via homography (`cv2.findHomography` + `decompose_homography`) into `(R_b, t_b)`, and these become teacher targets. Forward-backward consistency on the flow produces a per-pair pixel residual `σ_b_px` that is used to weight the distillation term.
2. Speed-diverse training — 4 shape families × 2 speeds each, peak speeds spanning {0.1, 0.15, 0.2, 0.4, 0.5} m/s.

### 4.2 Configuration (`configs/step_k.yaml`)

- Train: `312-319` (8 trajs: circle ×2, figure_8 ×2, diamond ×2, zigzag ×2 at varied speeds).
- Val: `320` (simple_ellipse @ 0.3 m/s).
- Minitest: `322` (simple_triangle @ 0.3 m/s).
- Test: `[320, 321, 322, 323, 324]` — held-out shapes (ellipse, triangle, sinusoid_30s) at seen and unseen speeds. Traj `324` is a never-seen-during-training sinusoid_30s shape.
- All trajectories on `tex_03.webp`.
- Loss adds `TDirectDistillLoss` with `λ=1.0`, `σ_eps_t=0.001`, `σ_eps_R=0.005`.

### 4.3 RAFT precompute outcome (`step_k_results/raft_precompute_summary.json`)

| traj | valid_fraction | mean_σ_px (valid pairs only) | mean_flow_px |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 312 | 0.0000 | NaN | 0.151 |
| 313 | 0.00067 | 0.0374 | 0.652 |
| 314 | 0.0000 | NaN | 0.161 |
| 315 | 0.0000 | NaN | 0.573 |
| 316 | 0.00067 | 0.0371 | 0.328 |
| 317 | 0.00100 | 0.0347 | 0.647 |
| 318 | 0.00067 | 0.0324 | 0.335 |
| 319 | 0.00400 | 0.0227 | 0.795 |
| 320 | 0.00067 | 0.0248 | 0.395 |

`valid_fraction` is the share of frame-pairs that passed forward-backward consistency. Across all 9 cached trajectories the rate is **0.0% to 0.4%** — RAFT marked 99.6%-100.0% of pairs as invalid. The mean flow magnitude is 0.15-0.80 px per pair, far below the 2 px RANSAC threshold used in `cv2.findHomography`. Where RAFT's σ was finite, the median was 0.022-0.037 px — the FB-consistency threshold rejected pairs whose disagreement was an order of magnitude smaller than this, suggesting the threshold was effectively unreachable on these uniform-texture short-baseline images.

**Consequence.** The distillation term carries `valid_mask` as a multiplier in `models/losses_distill.py::TDirectDistillLoss`. With `valid_mask` near zero on every batch, the distillation loss contributed essentially zero gradient. Any V improvement in Step K vs Step J is attributable to the speed-diverse training data alone — the RAFT distillation intervention did not engage. We did not retest with relaxed FB thresholds or with a different scene type within the timeframe of this work; we report the negative result and revisit it in §10.

The Step K test set covers ellipse, triangle, and sinusoid_30s — three shape families *not* in training. To test whether V's within-family generalisation extends to held-out instances of the four *trained* families (figure_8, circle, diamond, zigzag), we added trajectories `325-328` (each a held-out seed at an intermediate unseen speed of 0.25-0.30 m/s) and ran `slurm/step_k_extended_eval.sh`.

## 5. Step K cross-family — apparent improvement is a SE3-alignment artifact

### 5.1 Apparent result (`step_k_crossfamily_results/results.csv`)

| Method | 3 | 100 | 101 | 102 | mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| V  | 2.920 | 4.615 | 4.060 | 2.860 | **3.614** |
| I  | 4.346 | 4.413 | 3.223 | 3.762 | 3.936 |
| VI | 3.201 | 4.888 | 3.333 | 2.837 | 3.565 |

V_avg dropped from Step J's 10.85 m to 3.61 m on the same four cross-family trajs — naively a 3× cross-family improvement.

### 5.2 Investigation

Follow-up investigation revealed three independent diagnostics that disagree with the naive interpretation. We ran each on the TUM trajectory files in `step_j_cross_results/eval/trajectories/` and `step_k_crossfamily_results/eval/trajectories/`.

### 5.3 Diagnostic 1 — Sim3 vs SE3 alignment

We recomputed alignment with both SE3 (rotation + translation, no scale; this is what `evaluate_tdirect.py` reports as `ate_aligned`) and Sim3 (with a single scalar scale). For every (method, trajectory) pair, the Sim3-aligned RMSE is numerically identical between J and K. The Sim3 scale factor is what differs, and it differs because K's predictions are slightly less collapsed in magnitude than J's.

| traj | meth | J SE3 | K SE3 | J Sim3 | K Sim3 | J scale | K scale |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3   | v  | 12.236 | 2.920 | **2.567** | **2.567** | 0.006 | 0.051 |
| 3   | vi | 13.707 | 3.201 | **2.567** | **2.567** | 0.005 | 0.035 |
| 100 | v  | 10.653 | 4.615 | **4.357** | **4.358** | 0.020 | 0.110 |
| 100 | vi |  9.899 | 4.888 | **4.357** | **4.357** | 0.021 | 0.085 |
| 101 | v  |  8.461 | 4.060 | **2.038** | **2.036** | 0.022 | 0.053 |
| 101 | vi |  8.180 | 3.333 | **2.039** | **2.038** | 0.020 | 0.063 |
| 102 | v  | 12.045 | 2.860 | **1.698** | **1.697** | 0.005 | 0.033 |
| 102 | vi | 11.175 | 2.837 | **1.698** | **1.698** | 0.005 | 0.031 |

Both J and K predictions need to be **upscaled by 4×–200×** to match GT magnitude. K's predictions need slightly less rescaling than J's. The SE3 ATE difference is captured entirely by this scale dimension; once Sim3 absorbs it, the two are indistinguishable.

### 5.4 Diagnostic 2 — Per-step magnitude

For each cross-family trajectory we computed per-frame `‖Δp‖` (consecutive-frame translation norm) for GT, V/J, and V/K:

| traj | GT mean | V/J mean (ratio to GT) | V/K mean (ratio to GT) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 3   | 0.0263 | 0.0045 (0.17) | 0.0011 (0.04) |
| 100 | 0.0182 | 0.0038 (0.21) | 0.0013 (0.07) |
| 101 | 0.0110 | 0.0036 (0.33) | 0.0025 (0.23) |
| 102 | 0.0182 | 0.0066 (0.36) | 0.0017 (0.09) |

V predictions in K are **3-6× smaller per step than V predictions in J**, and **4-25× smaller than GT**. K predicts less motion than J, not better motion. Both networks default to near-zero predictions on out-of-distribution sequences; K is just slightly less collapsed.

### 5.5 Diagnostic 3 — Direction agreement

Mean angular error between predicted-velocity and GT-velocity vectors per consecutive-frame pair:

## 6. I network failure analysis

### 6.1 Observation

I_avg = 1.242 m on the Step K within-family test set (`step_k_results/results.csv`). Visually, I network predictions are near-stationary or near-straight-line traces rather than recognisable trajectory shapes (`step_k_results/eval/plots/i_<tid>_3d.png`, `i_<tid>_topdown.png`).

### 6.2 Per-step magnitude diagnostic

For each test trajectory we computed per-frame `‖Δp‖` for GT and I prediction, and the angular error between GT and I per-frame velocity:

| traj | shape | GT mean | I mean | I / GT | I dir mean |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 320 | ellipse mid     | 0.00243 | 0.00036 | 0.15 | 80.6° |
| 321 | ellipse fast    | 0.00370 | 0.00037 | 0.10 | 74.6° |
| 322 | triangle mid    | 0.00267 | 0.00085 | 0.32 | 56.3° |
| 323 | triangle fast   | 0.00435 | 0.00110 | 0.25 | 57.6° |
| 324 | sinusoid_30s    | 0.00340 | 0.00112 | 0.33 | 25.7° |

I predicts 10-33% of GT motion magnitude. Direction is informative on the most aggressive trajectory (traj 324, sinusoid, 25.7°) but degrades to random on smooth-curvature ellipses (~80°). Magnitude is the dominant deficit, not direction.

### 6.3 Failure mode classification: NEAR-ZERO BIAS COLLAPSE

Two compounding causes:

**(a) Loss formulation.** I is trained with `IMUPoseLoss` (`models/losses.py` line 190), `L = w_rot · L_rot + w_trans · L_t`, where `L_t = (t_pred - t_gt).abs().sum(-1).mean()` — L1 over 3 axes, mean over batch. **No hover-weighting.** Per-frame translation magnitudes on Step K training data are ~3 mm (each 10 ms image-pair interval at peak training speed of 0.5 m/s gives 5 mm; mean is closer to 3 mm). Predicting zero gives `L_t ≈ |t_gt|.sum() ≈ 0.003`. Predicting half-truth gives `L_t = 0.5 · |t_gt|.sum() ≈ 0.0015` — better — but in mixed batches with both small and large samples, the dominant contribution to the L1 mean comes from the large samples, and the gradient pushes toward zero on the small ones because their target is so close to zero. V's loss (`TDirectPoseLoss`) avoids this with a sigmoid hovering weight that down-weights samples with small `|t_gt|`.

**(b) Architectural noise floor.** I uses 10 ms IMU windows (10 samples at 1000 Hz), exactly the inter-image-frame interval. Per-window translation signal is ~3 mm. Gravity-integration noise over the same window is `0.5 · g · dt² = 0.5 · 9.81 · 0.0001 = 0.5 mm`. Signal-to-noise ratio is ~6:1 on a good window, ~1:1 on a small-motion window. There is no inter-window state — each window is processed independently by a 2-layer LSTM with 128 hidden units and produces (R, t) for that 10 ms slice. Modern learned-IMU approaches (TLIO, RoNIN, AirIO) operate on 200-1000 sample windows (200 ms - 1 s), 20-100× longer than ours, with substantially better SNR via the longer integration baseline. Our current setup is essentially at the noise floor for the task.

### 6.4 Phase 1 fix attempt — loss formulation

We added `IMUPoseLoss_hover` to `models/losses.py` (after the existing `IMUPoseLoss`, which was left untouched because `VILoss_tdirect` still uses it). The new loss mirrors `TDirectPoseLoss`'s translation handling: down-weights samples with small `|t_gt|` via the same `hovering_weight()` sigmoid (centre 0.01, width 0.005). `scripts/train_i.py` was modified to import and instantiate `IMUPoseLoss_hover` with the existing `loss.hover_mask_center` / `loss.hover_mask_width` config keys.

Smoke test (random tensors, CPU): on a homogeneous batch, the new loss produces identical totals to the old (per-sample weights are equal so cancel in normalisation). On a heterogeneous batch (50% `|t|=0.0001 m`, 50% `|t|=0.05 m`), predict-zero pays a 76% larger penalty under the new loss than under the old (0.136 vs 0.077). Perfect prediction has identical loss across both. Gradient backward produces finite values throughout. Hover-weight mean is monotone in `|t_gt|` (0.12 at `|t|=0.0001 m` → 1.00 at `|t|=0.1 m`).

The retrain SLURM job was submitted (`slurm/step_k_phase1_i_eval.sh` chained on `slurm/step_k_train_i.sh`); the eval script backs up `outputs/step_k/i/` to `outputs/step_k_phase0_baseline_eval/`, deletes the appendable `results.csv`, re-evals V/I/VI on Step K test set + cross-family, and prints a baseline-vs-Phase-1 comparison plus a decision-tree verdict. Results pending — **[job ID 1998879]**. The decision tree codified in that script:

- I_avg < 0.6 m → STOP (loss fix sufficient).
- I_avg in [0.6, 1.0] → MARGINAL.
- I_avg ≥ 1.0 m → PROCEED to Phase 2 (architecture fix).

### 6.5 Phase 2 fix — architecture (planned, not executed)

A plan was developed for replacing the 10 ms IMU window with a 100 ms causal context window (window ends at frame `b`, looking back 100 IMU samples). This requires changes to `utils/dataloader.py` (per-image-pair index unchanged; IMU slice extended), `configs/step_k.yaml` (new `data.imu_context_length` key), and routing in `models/network_vi*.py` (decision: keep VI's IMU at 10 samples and route the longer context only to the I branch, vs accept that VI also gets the longer context and retrain VI). The cleaner option preserves V/VI checkpoints; either way I retrains. Not executed pending Phase 1's verdict.

## 8. Findings

### 8.1 Within-family generalisation works with speed-diverse training

V achieved 0.184 m mean ATE on a held-out test set covering ellipse, triangle, and sinusoid_30s shapes at speeds 0.3-0.5 m/s, with `seg2s_median` 0.026-0.105 m (centimetre-scale per-frame drift). On the never-seen sinusoid_30s shape (traj 324, controlled 0.5 m/s), V achieved 0.144 m. This is shape-novelty generalisation when motion magnitude is in the training distribution.

### 8.2 Within-family generalisation across the 4 trained shape families

V achieves 0.078 m mean ATE (sub-decimetre per-trajectory: 0.066, 0.068, 0.084, 0.095 m) on held-out instances of the four trained shape families (figure_8, circle, diamond, zigzag) at intermediate unseen speeds (0.25-0.30 m/s, between the trained speed bands), with `seg2s_median` 0.022-0.036 m. V's within-family generalisation is broad across the 4 trained shape families, not specific to the ellipse/triangle/sinusoid_30s test cases.

VI is heterogeneous on the extended set (mean 0.081 m, helps on 2/4, hurts on 2/4). The VI gain seen on Step K's original test set does not carry over uniformly to held-out instances of trained shapes.

### 8.3 Cross-family generalisation to aggressive 100 s trajectories remains unsolved

The apparent 3× ATE improvement of Step K (3.61 m) over Step J (10.85 m) on cross-family trajectories is an SE3 alignment artifact. Sim3-aligned RMSE is numerically identical between J and K on every (method, trajectory) pair. Per-step magnitudes are 4-25× smaller than GT in both. `seg2s_median` is 1.0-2.5 m in both. Direction agreement is ~90° (random) in both. Both networks default to predicting near-zero motion on out-of-distribution sequences.

### 8.4 Fusion adds value within-family for the first time

VI < V on the Step K within-family test set (0.173 vs 0.184 m), with VI better on 4 of 5 trajectories (ranges from -19% on traj 321 to -1% on traj 323; +15% regression on traj 322). In every prior step (F, G, H, I, J) VI was either equal to or worse than V. The improvement is attributable to (a) V being good enough that fusion has a sensible signal to mix with I, and (b) V/I being frozen across both VI stages from Step J onward (`stage2_lr_mult: 0.0`), which prevented the silent-fine-tuning issue that contaminated Step I's fusion measurement.

### 8.5 RAFT distillation did not engage

RAFT marked 99.6%-100.0% of frame-pairs as invalid via forward-backward consistency rejection across all 9 cached trajectories (`step_k_results/raft_precompute_summary.json`). The mean flow magnitude was 0.15-0.80 px per pair, and FB-consistency thresholds rejected pairs whose disagreement was ~0.02-0.04 px median. The distillation loss term `valid_mask · 1/(σ² + ε²) · ‖t_a − t_b‖²` was effectively zeroed by the mask. The Step K improvements over Step J on within-family are attributable to speed-diverse training data (4 shape families × 2 speeds vs Step J's 3 families × 1 speed) alone.

### 8.6 I network exhibits near-zero bias collapse

Per-step magnitudes are 10-33% of GT across the Step K within-family test set. The dominant cause is L1 loss without hover-weighting on a per-frame translation signal of ~3 mm; "predict zero" is locally near-optimal. A secondary cause is the 10 ms IMU window placing the per-window translation signal within an order of magnitude of gravity-integration noise. Phase 1 fix (`IMUPoseLoss_hover`) was implemented and submitted for retraining; result pending. Phase 2 fix (longer IMU windows, TLIO/AirIO-style 100-1000 ms context) is planned but not executed.

## 9. Limitations and open questions

### 9.1 Synthetic-only

All experiments use Blender-rendered scenes on a uniform-textured ground plane at constant 4 m altitude. Real-world deployment requires additional validation we did not perform.

### 9.2 Cross-family scope

We did not test interventions that decompose trajectory length (30 s vs 100 s) from motion amplitude (0.5 m/s vs aggressive sum-of-sinusoid) separately. Step K training was entirely 30 s; cross-family eval was entirely 100 s. Whether the cross-family failure is driven by length, amplitude, or shape is undetermined within the experiments run.

### 9.3 RAFT distillation negative result

RAFT pretrained on Sintel/KITTI did not provide a usable training signal on uniform carpet textures with low motion magnitudes. We did not test (a) RAFT on textured scenes with stronger feature density, (b) relaxed FB-consistency thresholds, or (c) alternative teacher signals (e.g. SuperPoint correspondences). The Step K hypothesis as stated remains untested; we only confirmed that the chosen mechanism did not engage.

### 9.4 I network architecture not fixed

We modified the loss in scope (Phase 1, `IMUPoseLoss_hover`); the architectural fix (longer windows) follows TLIO/AirIO precedent and is documented as future work in §10. Even if Phase 1 helps, the per-window SNR is bounded by the 10 ms slice; a loss change cannot improve information that is not in the input.

### 9.5 Sim3 vs SE3 diagnostic was applied only to Step K cross-family

We discovered the SE3-alignment-artifact failure mode in Step K. Steps F, G, H, I, and J cross-family eval (where it exists) were reported only with SE3 ATE; we did not retroactively run Sim3 on them. For full rigor every step's cross-family metric should be re-evaluated under Sim3 to identify which earlier "improvements" are similarly inflated by alignment.

### 9.6 One pending SLURM job

`step_k_phase1_i_eval` (job 1998879, hover-loss I retrain + eval) had not produced local output at writing time. Section §6.4 is explicitly marked pending. `step_k_extended` and `step_k_traineval` have completed and their results populate §4.5, §4.6, and §8.2.

## 10. Future work

10.1 **Replace 10 ms IMU windows with 100-1000 ms causal context windows** (TLIO/AirIO convention). This is the indicated fix for I if Phase 1's loss change does not bring I_avg below 0.6 m. Implementation sketch in §6.5; primary risk is VI compatibility (whether to route the longer context to both V/VI's IMU branches or only to standalone I).

10.2 **Decompose length vs magnitude in cross-family.** Train on 30 s simple shapes (Step K's setup), evaluate on intermediate-aggressive 50 s trajectories at varying peak accelerations. This would distinguish whether cross-family failure is a length problem or a motion-magnitude problem.

10.3 **Test RAFT distillation under conditions where it can engage.** Render a textured scene (carpet patterns with stronger feature density, or a textured plane at lower altitude) and re-precompute. Alternatively relax the FB-consistency threshold in `configs/step_k.yaml` (`raft.fb_valid_threshold_px`, currently 5.0 px — but RAFT is rejecting pairs at sub-0.04 px disagreement, so the rejection mechanism is somewhere else; investigate first).

10.4 **Apply Sim3 vs SE3 diagnostic to Steps F-J.** Compute Sim3-aligned RMSE on every cross-family TUM file from Steps F, G, H, J. Identify which earlier "improvements" are similarly captured by the scale dimension and which represent real per-frame recovery.

10.5 **Evaluate the gate behaviour from Step I more carefully.** `outputs/step_i/eval/gate_stats.json` (when available) records mean α_t / α_R, ρ_v statistics, and `corr(ρ_v, ‖t_v − t_gt‖)`. Re-run on Step K's data to test whether the soft-fusion architecture (which we abandoned in favour of residual fusion in Step H) would now have a discriminative signal to use, given that V is finally accurate.

## Appendix A — Bug log

A.1 **Step F → G: train/test texture mismatch.** Step F used different textures across train and test trajectories. Confounded shape generalisation with appearance generalisation. Fix: render all trajectories on `tex_03.webp` into `data/raw_g/`. Outcome: V_traj_3 dropped 7.41 → 5.29.

A.2 **Step G → H: (R, t/d, n) decomposition.** V predicted scale-free direction `t/d` and a plane normal `n`, with metric translation reconstructed at use-time as `t = (t/d) · d`. The per-frame altitude multiplication and the indirect plane-normal supervision were a 27% bug. Fix (`Code/Phase 2/outputs/step_h/CHANGES.md`): direct `(R, t)` prediction in `NetworkV_tdirect`; loss drops `L_n`, `L_warp`, `L_d`, `L_td`. Outcome: V_traj_3 dropped 5.29 → 3.86.

A.3 **Step H → I: gate collapse in anchored fusion.** `NetworkVI_anchored` introduced soft fusion gates with photometric self-validation. The gates collapsed to constants (`mean α_t ≈ 0.99`, `mean α_R ≈ 0.02`) and `corr(ρ_v, ‖t_v − t_gt‖)` was approximately zero. The photometric residual carried no discriminative signal. Step J abandoned anchored fusion in favour of the residual-refiner architecture introduced in Step H.

A.4 **Step I → J: silent fine-tuning of V/I in VI Stage 2.** Step I used `stage2_lr_mult: 0.25`, which silently fine-tuned V and I during VI training and contaminated the fusion measurement. Fix (Step J onward, `step_j_results/CHANGES.md` Lesson L1): `stage2_lr_mult: 0.0`. Adam with `lr=0` is a no-op weight update; backbones remain in the optimiser per the lesson's "L10" note but do not move.

A.5 **Step J: train/test parametric overlap and `ate_aligned` hiding raw error.** All Step J test trajectories were drawn from the same parametric family as training, with only seed differing. Closest train-test L2 distance ~0.063 m, same magnitude as the reported ATE. `ate_aligned` (Umeyama) absorbed 2.7× the raw RMSE on average. We chose to live with the inflated number rather than retrain, but flagged it in §3.4 and added the genuinely-held-out V_avg = 0.098 m for traj subset (300, 306, 307).

A.6 **Step J: validation traj also in test set.** Traj 305 was both `val_traj_id` (driving `best.pt`) and a test traj. ~10% inflation on V_avg. Step K kept the val traj (320) in the test set as well, but added 4 additional held-out test trajs (321-324) to dilute the effect.

A.7 **Step K: RAFT precompute valid_fraction near zero.** RAFT marked 99.6-100.0% of pairs as invalid. Diagnosed in §4.3. Not "fixed" in the sense of getting RAFT to engage — we accepted that Step K is a speed-diversity ablation, not a distillation experiment, and reported the negative result.
